2026-09-21 · ​@Someone
1. Executive summary
The data-readiness phase is done, and the first GPU training batch (12 runs) is running on
Kaggle as of 21 Sep 2026. On the way we changed the paper's claim. We are no longer
proposing "a new lightweight Transformer U-Net". The claim is now: standard polyp-
segmentation benchmarks overstate progress, and we measure by how much.
Three facts found in our own data drove the change:
Video leakage. The CVC-ClinicDB test frames (62) come from the same colonoscopy
videos as the 550 training frames. Our earlier train/val split also put adjacent video
frames on both sides.
Dataset overlap. PolypDB contains Kvasir-SEG images, including Kvasir test images.
ColonDB and CVC-300 share 58 identical images.
No false-alarm measurement. The five standard test sets contain only polyp frames.
No one reports how often a model "sees" a polyp in healthy colon.
The paper will measure these effects with controlled experiments: 4 models, 3 seeds, a
video-grouped split, 4,275 polyp-free frames and a 5-fold ClinicDB leakage study. Our own
model (ChakraXAttnUNet) stays in the comparison, but it is not the headline contribution.
Section 3 explains why.
2. Failure analysis: what we tried before, and why it did not work
Every earlier model either underperformed a 2020–2021 published baseline or was
measured on a split we can no longer trust. The numbers below come from our own

2.1 ChakraTransformer (the main failure)
Wrong architecture for dense prediction. A plain ViT with 16-pixel patches sees a 384
px image as one 24×24 grid. A small polyp covers 1–2 tokens, and there are no multi-
scale features. Every strong polyp model (PraNet, Polyp-PVT, SSFormer, CFA-Net) uses
a hierarchical encoder plus a dedicated decoder.
Far too many parameters for the data. 309M parameters were trained on about 1,000
images. The literature review we compiled (ColonFormer's MiT-B1 to B5 scaling)
reports accuracy falling beyond mid-sized encoders on four of five test sets.
The decoder was an afterthought. About 2M parameters of Conv+Deconv produced
the whole mask.
The evaluation protocol was not comparable to published work. Kvasir was scored
on "the last 10%", and ClinicDB on "all 495" frames. ClinicDB has 612 frames and the
standard test set is 62. The training split is unknown, so overlap with the test frames
cannot be excluded. Even U-Net (2015) beat it on ClinicDB.
Speed was also a problem. 7.8 FPS at FP32 on one T4, about 27 FPS at FP16.

2.2 The 128 px experiments
Resolution. 128 px is about 7.6× fewer pixels than the 352 px standard, which erases
small polyps. The ± 0.21 standard deviation says a sizeable share of images failed
almost completely.
From-scratch training does not work at this data size. Every model without
ImageNet pre-training collapsed (0.28–0.77). Architecture changes without pre-
training are not a lever here.
Kvasir-SEG only, random 70/15/15 split. Nothing was checked for duplicates, and
nothing was tested on unseen hospitals.
2.3 Can ChakraTransformer be revived? No.
Every fix in our own report (a hierarchical encoder, a reverse-attention decoder, the
standard 1,450-image training set) turns it into Polyp-PVT. The best case is matching a
2021 model with 10× the parameters. That is weeks of GPU time for no new claim. What we
keep from it: the evaluation harness, FP16/TTA inference code, the FPS benchmarking, and
this negative result as a short discussion paragraph.
2.4 The ChakraXAttnUNet design (a second agent's proposal)
It uses a Swin-Tiny encoder with cross-attention skip fusion, and is 28.2M parameters, so
not lightweight. v1 had a real design flaw: each skip feature was average-pooled to 16×16
before attention, and the full-resolution skip was never passed on. That throws away the
fine detail that edges and small polyps need. We fixed it in v2 by adding a direct full-
resolution skip path (28.6M params). Its novelty is still thin: close to Swin-Unet,
UCTransNet and TransFuse. Our literature review found that such modules add about
0.005–0.015 Dice in published ablations, which is below seed-to-seed noise.
3. Why we chose this direction
We chose the direction where our evidence is strongest and hardest to dismiss. A new
attention block competes with 30+ published variants whose gains are often below
measurement noise. A measured flaw in the benchmark everyone uses applies to all of
them.

7. Experimental protocol (Stage 1)
Every model is trained and tested under one fixed protocol, so that differences come
from the model or the split and not from the training recipe. Code:
M:\chakramodelpro\leakbench (Kaggle dataset gokulrocky/chakra-leakbench-v1 ,
private).
7.1 Fixed training recipe
352×352 input; AdamW (lr 1e-4, weight decay 1e-4); cosine schedule; 50 epochs; batch
size 16; mixed precision. Loss: the PraNet structure loss (boundary-weighted BCE +
weighted IoU). Augmentation: flips, 90° rotations, mild brightness and contrast jitter.
Checkpoint = best mean validation Dice. Tests run once, on that frozen checkpoint.

7.3 Metrics
Accuracy: mean Dice and mean IoU per image, with the prediction resized to the
original ground-truth resolution (standard PraNet evaluation). MAE is also recorded.
Small polyps: mDice on images whose polyp covers less than 5% of the frame.
False alarms: the share of the 4,275 polyp-free frames with any predicted polyp area
(thresholds 0%, 0.1%, 1%).
Efficiency: parameters, FPS and latency at batch size 1 in FP16, and peak VRAM, all on
the same Kaggle T4.
Reporting: each test set separately, labelled seen or unseen domain; mean ± SD over 3
seeds.
7.4 Experiments (38 runs, about 20 T4-hours)
E3 design detail: both fold sets partition the same 612 frames with identical fold sizes
(216/96/103/102/95). Pooled Dice is therefore over the same frames, and the gap isolates
leakage. Model selection uses Kvasir-only validation, so ClinicDB never influences
checkpoint choice.
