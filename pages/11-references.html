Computer-Aided Detection of Colorectal Polyps: A Randomized Controlled Trial

Repici, A., Badalamenti, M., Radford, R., et al.
Annals of Internal Medicine, 2020, Vol. 173, No. 8, pp. 593–600.
#01

Landmark European multicenter randomized controlled trial (685 patients) demonstrating that real-time AI significantly increases adenoma detection rate (ADR 54.8% vs 40.4%, p < 0.001) primarily driven by diminutive polyps.

Clinical RCT DOI: 10.7326/M20-1994 PubMed: 32866032

Real-time automatic detection system increases colonoscopic polyp and adenoma detection rates: a prospective randomised controlled study

Wang, P., Berzin, T.M., Glissen Brown, J.R., et al.
Gut, 2019, Vol. 68, No. 10, pp. 1813–1819.
#02

Pioneering open-label randomized trial (1,058 patients) establishing that real-time CADe substantially boosts ADR (29.1% vs 20.3%, p < 0.001) without prolonging mucosal withdrawal inspection time.

Clinical RCT DOI: 10.1136/gutjnl-2018-317500 PubMed: 30814121

Real-time computer-aided detection of colorectal polyps during colonoscopy: a multicentre, randomized, tandem study

Hassan, C., Badalamenti, M., Maselli, R., et al.
Gut, 2021, Vol. 70, No. 8, pp. 1501–1508.
#03

Multicenter tandem randomized trial proving that CADe reduces Adenoma Miss Rate (AMR) by more than half (13.8% with CADe vs 32.4% with standard colonoscopy alone).

Tandem AMR Trial DOI: 10.1136/gutjnl-2020-323608 PubMed: 33328224

Impact of Artificial Intelligence on Miss Rate of Colorectal Neoplasia: A Multi-Center, Tandem Colonoscopy Randomized Clinical Trial

Wallace, M.B., Sharma, P., Bhandari, P., et al.
Gastroenterology, 2022, Vol. 163, No. 1, pp. 295–304.e5.
#04

Global multicenter tandem trial (United States, Europe) demonstrating that computer-aided detection slashes polyp miss rates across both expert and community gastroenterologists (20.1% vs 31.2%, p < 0.001).

Global Tandem RCT DOI: 10.1053/j.gastro.2022.03.048 PubMed: 35367468

Real-Time Use of Artificial Intelligence in Identification of Diminutive Polyps During Colonoscopy: A Prospective Study

Mori, Y., Kudo, S.E., Misawa, M., et al.
Annals of Internal Medicine, 2020, Vol. 172, No. 10, pp. 700–701.
#05

Clinical validation of AI characterization (CADx) in identifying diminutive rectosigmoid adenomas, achieving >90% negative predictive value required for the PIVI "resect-and-discard" strategy.

CADx Optical Biopsy DOI: 10.7326/M19-3248 PubMed: 32092770

New artificial intelligence-based technology for colonoscopy: European Society of Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ESGE) Position Statement

Hassan, C., Antonelli, G., Dumonceau, J.M., et al.
Endoscopy, 2021, Vol. 53, No. 3, pp. 284–296.
#06

Official ESGE guidelines recommending the clinical implementation of CADe systems for screening colonoscopy, with strict performance minimums for mucosal inspection and adenoma detection.

Clinical Guideline DOI: 10.1055/a-1387-0311 PubMed: 33596594

Endoscopist adenoma detection performance after cessation of real-time computer-aided detection: a retrospective multicentre study

Budzyński, P., et al.
The Lancet Gastroenterology & Hepatology, 2025, Vol. 10, No. 8, pp. 720–729.

Crucial multicenter evidence documenting cognitive deskilling: unassisted adenoma detection rates dropped by an absolute -6.0% among endoscopists after CADe was withdrawn, demonstrating clinician dependency.

Deskilling Audit DOI: 10.1016/S2468-1253(25)00142-8

De Novo Classification Order for GI Genius: Evaluation of Automatic Class III Designation (DEN200055)

US Food and Drug Administration (FDA)
FDA Medical Device Databases, 2021, 21 CFR 876.1510, Product Code QVD.

FDA Clearance FDA Decision Summary

Adenoma Detection Rate and Risk of Colorectal Cancer and Fatal Interval Cancer

Corley, D.A., Jensen, C.D., Marks, A.R., et al.
New England Journal of Medicine, 2014, Vol. 370, No. 14, pp. 1298–1306.
#09

Landmark longitudinal cohort study of 314,845 screening colonoscopies performed by 136 gastroenterologists establishing the epidemiological foundation of colonoscopy quality: each 1.0% absolute increase in adenoma detection rate (ADR) confers a 3.0% reduction in interval colorectal cancer incidence and a 5.0% reduction in fatal colorectal cancer mortality.

Clinical Epidemiology DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa1309086 PubMed: 24693890
