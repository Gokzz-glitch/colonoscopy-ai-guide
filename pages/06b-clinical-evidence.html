Pivotal Trials

The Landmark Randomized Controlled Trials

Following proof-of-concept retrospective deep learning prototypes in 2018, the field transitioned to prospective human randomized trials at an unprecedented pace.

Wang et al. (Gut 2019): The First Prospective RCT

Historic First

Gut 2019; 68:1813-1819 • Renmin Hospital of Wuhan University, China

The historic hinge point for medical computer vision: 1,058 consecutive patients were randomized 1:1 to real-time CADe assistance (n=522) versus standard diagnostic colonoscopy (n=536).

Standard Colonoscopy ADR: 20.3%
CADe-Assisted ADR: 29.1% (p < 0.001)
Absolute gain: +8.8% ADR. The entire increase was driven by diminutive polyps (≤5 mm); polyps ≥10 mm showed identical detection rates.

Repici et al. (Gastroenterology 2020): GI Genius

FDA Pivotal Trial

Gastroenterology 2020; 159:1999-2008 • Multi-Center Italian Trial (Humanitas, Milan)

Pivotal randomized trial evaluating Medtronic / Cosmo Pharmaceuticals GI Genius across 685 average-risk screening and surveillance patients.

Control ADR (Standard): 40.4%
GI Genius ADR: 54.8% (p < 0.001)
Adenomas Per Colonoscopy (APC): 1.07 vs 0.71 (p < 0.001)
Provided the pivotal regulatory evidence leading directly to the US FDA De Novo clearance (DEN200055) in April 2021.

The Clinical Paradox

The AGA 2025 Microsimulation Model & Diminutive Trade-offs

"The gains concentrate in diminutive polyps that were unlikely to become cancer, while the endpoint anyone cares about — CRC incidence and mortality — remains unmeasured."

The Diminutive Dilemma

Across 41 RCTs, CADe increases ADR by 20% relative (RR 1.20). However, device-specific meta-analyses (such as the 2025 GI Genius meta-analysis across 9,639 patients) prove that:

  • Advanced Adenoma Detection: Relative risk is RR 1.01 (95% CI 0.88–1.15) — completely unchanged. Large adenomas (≥10 mm) are rarely missed by human endoscopists.
  • Diminutive Polyp Concentration: Over 85% of incremental detections are tiny polyps (≤5 mm) carrying <0.1% individual malignancy risk.
  • Non-Neoplastic Resection Inflation: Resected polyps in the CADe group were 11% more likely to be non-neoplastic (34.0% non-neoplastic with CADe vs 28.8% in standard colonoscopy) because endoscopists resect harmless mucosal folds and bubbles flagged by AI bounding boxes.

Human Factors & Safety

Cognitive Deskilling: The ACCEPT Trial Finding (2025)

Can relying on an automated co-pilot degrade a physician's native diagnostic skills? In August 2025, The Lancet Gastroenterology & Hepatology published the first empirical evidence of medical AI deskilling.

Budzyń et al. (Lancet Gastro Hep, Aug 2025): The ACCEPT Trial

Four Certified Polish Screening Centers • 1,443 Non-AI Colonoscopies Evaluated
Alarming Safety Signal

Why Did ADR Decline by 6% Post-AI?

The authors frame this as the first empirical study suggesting AI exposure might negatively affect patient-relevant endpoints in medicine generally. Eye-tracking and cognitive ergonomics reveal two primary mechanisms:

1. Attentional Complacency & Cognitive Offloading

Clinicians become conditioned to passive vigilance—waiting for a green bounding box to flash rather than actively scrutinizing every subtle fold and mucosal pit.

2. Gaze Fixation Disruption

Eye-tracking studies demonstrate that endoscopists alter their saccadic search patterns, anchoring their gaze near screen boundaries waiting for system overlays, degrading native active scanning habits.

Regulatory Standards

ASGE PVI Thresholds & The 2025 Guideline Schism

How should healthcare systems regulate real-time optical diagnosis, and why did international clinical practice guidelines split four ways in 2025?

ASGE PVI Optical Diagnosis Benchmarks

Preserving and Incorporating Valuable endoscopic Innovations (PVI)

"Diagnose-and-Leave" Benchmark:

For diminutive (≤5 mm) rectosigmoid polyps, an optical diagnosis system must achieve a Negative Predictive Value (NPV) ≥90% for adenomatous histology before clinicians can safely leave benign hyperplastic lesions in place without biopsy.

"Resect-and-Discard" Benchmark:

For diminutive polyps throughout the colon, optical diagnosis combined with resection and disposal (bypassing laboratory histology) requires ≥90% concordance with formal pathology in assigning post-polypectomy surveillance intervals.

The Unprecedented 2025 Guideline Schism

In 2025, four major international medical bodies issued conflicting recommendations:

AGA (Sultan et al., Gastroenterology 2025): No recommendation for or against CADe. Cites very low certainty of evidence and close balance of benefits versus overdiagnosis burdens; panel split vote.
ESGE (Bretthauer et al., Endoscopy 2025): Weak recommendation in favor of routine CADe use during screening colonoscopies (13 of 19 voting experts supported).
BMJ / MAGIC Rapid Recommendations (2025): Recommendation AGAINST routine CADe use. Emphasizes ballooning pathology costs and lack of proof for CRC incidence reduction.
WEO Position Statement (2025): Cautiously positive; acknowledges surrogate gains but warns of short-term cost escalations and calls for definitive 10-year mortality trials.
