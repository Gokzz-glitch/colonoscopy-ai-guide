The Empirical Research Project

The ChakraModel
Research Project & Protocol

How a machine learning project pivoted from proposing another marginal transformer architecture to scientifically measuring and repairing benchmark inflation across colonoscopy AI.

Executive Summary

The Great Strategic Pivot

The ChakraModel project originally commenced in mid-2026 with an orthodox deep learning ambition: engineer a high-capacity vision transformer architecture capable of setting new SOTA numbers on standard polyp segmentation leaderboards.

However, rigorous empirical audits of our training logs and public datasets shattered that objective. We discovered that benchmark leaderboards were fundamentally contaminated: test sets leaked video frames from training sets, candidate datasets absorbed held-out test frames, and models were evaluated exclusively on polyps with zero testing on healthy mucosa.

💡
The ChakraModel Mission Statement

"Standard polyp-segmentation benchmarks overstate clinical progress, and we measure by how much."

Rather than publishing another incremental model claiming +0.005 Dice on compromised data, ChakraModel established leakbench: a unified, reproducible benchmarking platform measuring true out-of-distribution generalization, leak-free validation drift, and real-time inference viability.

Radical Scientific Transparency

Failure Analysis: What Didn't Work and Why

ChakraTransformer & 128px Collapse

Genuine scientific progress requires publishing negative results. Before converging on our final architecture and protocol, the project rigorously evaluated two flawed hypotheses: an over-parameterized Vision Transformer (ViT) and low-resolution from-scratch optimization.

1. ChakraTransformer (ViT-L/16 @ 384 px, 309.2M Parameters)

Catastrophic Underperformance

Evaluated on dual NVIDIA T4 GPUs on Kaggle (chakra_transformer_best.pth), ChakraTransformer coupled an ImageNet-21k pre-trained Vision Transformer Large encoder (307.2M params) with a minimal convolutional decoder head (2.0M params) at $384 \times 384$ input resolution.

Root Causes of ChakraTransformer's Failure:

❌ Non-Hierarchical Spatial Collapse

Standard ViT operates on non-overlapping $16 \times 16$ patches. At $384 \times 384$, the entire image collapses into a single $24 \times 24$ token grid. A diminutive polyp covers only 1 or 2 tokens, destroying high-frequency boundary cues.

❌ Severe Decoder Asymmetry

A 307M backbone was paired with a rudimentary 2M convolutional decoder. Without multi-scale feature pyramids (FPN) or reverse attention modules, the decoder could not translate abstract latent tokens back to crisp boundaries.

❌ Extreme Parameter Bloat (12x SOTA)

309 million parameters trained on ~1,000 medical images violates sample-efficiency principles. The network severely overfitted and discarded the local inductive biases that CNNs preserve.

❌ Complete Real-Time Failure (7.76 FPS)

At 7.76 FPS (FP32), the model fell drastically short of the 25 FPS clinical threshold required for live endoscopic video. Even with FP16, throughput capped at ~26 FPS with massive latency jitter.

1. Fixing the Fatal v1 Skip-Pooling Flaw

In our v1 prototype, encoder skip features were average-pooled to $\le 16 \times 16$ before calculating cross-attention. This eliminated computational bottlenecks but wiped out sub-millimeter pit patterns and mucosal margins.

The v2 Solution: Dual-Path Skip Routing routes features through two concurrent paths: a context-gated attention selection path plus an unpooled, direct $1 \times 1$ convolutionally projected skip bypass.

2. Auxiliary Morphological Boundary Supervision

During training, an auxiliary boundary head is supervised with the explicit mathematical morphology boundary target:

B(G) = MaxPool_3x3(G) - (-MaxPool_3x3(-G))

This forces intermediate feature layers to actively delineate lesion borders, preventing blurry predictions on flat Paris 0-IIa/IIb adenomas.

Standardized Experimental Setup

Stage 1 Protocol: leakbench

38 GPU Runs | ~20 NVIDIA T4-Hours

To eliminate experimental confounding, all models are evaluated in a unified PyTorch harness with locked hyperparameters, canonical PraNet structure loss, and multi-seed execution.

Boundary-Weighted Objective

Weighted BCE + Weighted IoU with dynamic edge weighting: $\omega_{ij} = 1 + 5 |\text{AvgPool}_{31\times31}(G)_{ij} - G_{ij}|$.

Histological Distribution & Clinical Value

PICCOLO includes complete histological ground truth: 50 tubular/tubulovillous adenomas, 17 non-neoplastic hyperplastic polyps, and 9 invasive adenocarcinomas. By evaluating models across these exact clinical strata, ChakraModel transitions medical computer vision from generic pixel overlap scoring to clinically meaningful diagnostic risk assessment.
