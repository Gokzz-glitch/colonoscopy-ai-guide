Commercial Technologies • Module 07

Commercial CADe & CADx Systems:
Profiles, Hardware & Clinical Reality

In real hospital endoscopy suites, clinicians do not run open-source PyTorch models. Examine the proprietary inline hardware appliances, strict <40 ms video latency budgets, and regulatory clearances (FDA De Novo, 510(k), CE Mark, PMDA) of all 6 dominant commercial platforms.

The Physical Hardware Reality

Commercial endoscopy AI systems exist as dedicated inline video appliances positioned between the endoscope Camera Control Unit (CCU) and the primary surgical display monitor. They capture uncompressed video streams via DVI, SDI, or HDMI, execute per-frame convolutional inference coupled with temporal tracking, and overlay bounding boxes onto the live feed under a strict real-time latency budget of <40 ms.

The Six Commercial Platforms in Clinical Use

Detailed clinical profiles, regulatory clearances, optical integrations, and trial evidence for each commercial device operating in hospitals worldwide.

Market Leader

Medtronic GI Genius™

Cosmo / Medtronic

Developed by Cosmo Pharmaceuticals (AI Medical Devices division, Italy) and commercialized globally by Medtronic. The historic first AI system to achieve US FDA clearance.

  • Regulatory Clearance: FDA De Novo (DEN200055, Apr 2021) • CE Mark (Oct 2019)
  • Clinical Function: CADe (Computer-Aided Detection)
  • Optical Modality: White Light Imaging (WLI) • Scope-Agnostic Inline Box
  • Inference Latency: <33 ms (60 FPS Native Pass-Through)
  • Pivotal RCTs: Repici et al. (Gastro 2020: ADR 54.8% vs 40.4%) • Wallace 2022
Clinical UI: Overlays a bright green rectangular bounding box around detected lesions; accompanied by an optional pleasant acoustic chime and foot-pedal freeze toggle to prevent alarm fatigue.
Dual Modality

Fujifilm CAD EYE™

Fujifilm Corporation

Tightly integrated into Fujifilm’s ELUXEO 7000/8000 endoscopy tower via the EX-1 expansion unit. Seamlessly unifies detection and characterization without scope changes.

  • Regulatory Clearance: CE Mark (Feb 2020) • Japan PMDA Approval (2020)
  • Clinical Function: Dual CADe (Detection) + CADx (Characterization)
  • Optical Modality: WLI, LCI (Linked Color Imaging), BLI (Blue Light Imaging)
  • Inference Latency: <33 ms (Real-time multi-light processing)
  • Pivotal RCTs: Neumann et al. (Endoscopy) • Japanese Multicenter BLI Trials
Clinical UI: CADe displays an animated detection circle/square; CADx automatically engages under BLI mode, displaying a dual-color pie chart distinguishing hyperplastic from neoplastic tissue based on NICE classification criteria.
Flagship Platform

Olympus ENDO-AID

Olympus / Odin Vision

The premier AI platform for the global endoscopy market leader, natively integrated into the flagship EVIS X1 video system. Algorithms powered by Olympus and Odin Vision (acquired Dec 2022).

  • Regulatory Clearance: CE Mark (Nov 2020) • Japanese PMDA Approval
  • Clinical Function: CADe (Detection) + CADx (Characterization)
  • Optical Modality: WLI, NBI (Narrow Band 415/540nm), TXI, RDI
  • Inference Latency: <30 ms (4K and Full HD pass-through at 60 FPS)
  • Pivotal RCTs: Hassan et al. (Gut 2021) • European Multicenter RCT Series cutting AMR
Clinical UI: Overlays a dynamic white/blue bounding marker that snaps to lesion contours; audio feedback; instant handle button toggle; seamless EVIS X1 report generator with Kudo pit pattern matching.
Ultra-Fast Edge

NEC WISE VISION®

NEC Corporation

Engineered by Japanese IT giant NEC Corporation, leveraging decades of facial recognition pattern-matching and ultra-fast feature extraction algorithms adapted for mucosal textures.

  • Regulatory Clearance: CE Mark (Dec 2020) • Japan PMDA Approval (2020)

  • Optical Modality: WLI & Chromoendoscopy • Universal Vendor Connectivity

  • Pivotal RCTs: Japanese Screening Center Trials (Mori et al.)

Hardware Engineering

Hardware Implementation & The <40 ms Latency Constraint

Why is latency in colonoscopy AI a hard physiological limit rather than an engineering preference?

The Motion-to-Photon Latency Budget

During withdrawal inspection, the endoscopist continuously torques the scope shaft, turns steering wheels, and flexes the tip. This creates rapid optical movement across the monitor.

If the AI inference pipeline introduces more than 40 to 50 milliseconds of delay:

  • Visual-Proprioceptive Mismatch: The physician moves the steering wheel, but the screen image (or bounding box) lags behind. This causes nausea and visual disorientation analogous to simulator sickness.
  • Bounding Box Dragging: When the scope rapidly pans across a fold, a high-latency bounding box visually drags behind the real physical lesion, overlaying empty mucosa.
  • Clinical Consequence: Frustrated clinicians immediately switch off the device, rendering the AI useless.

Scientific Transparency

The Academic vs Commercial Divide: The Trade Secret Wall

Why can't researchers read the "GI Genius architecture paper"? Understanding the sharp divergence between academic publishing and commercial device manufacturing.

What is Publicly Disclosed

  • Clinical Trial Design: Number of patients, randomization protocols, ADR, APC, and withdrawal times are meticulously published in top journals (Gastroenterology, Gut, Lancet).
  • Hardware Latency: Pass-through frame rates (60 FPS) and electrical safety certifications (IEC 60601-1).
  • Regulatory Summary Data: FDA 510(k) decision summaries provide high-level sensitivity on retrospective test sets and bounding box display guidelines.

What Remains a Proprietary Secret

  • Neural Backbone: Whether systems utilize optimized ResNets, proprietary CNNs, or custom vision transformers is undisclosed.
  • Training Set Composition: The exact hospitals, patient counts, demographic mixtures, and edge-case samples used to train weights are held as trade secrets.
  • Loss Formulation & Calibration: How models penalize false positives on bubbles vs missing an SSL is never published.
  • Temporal Tracking Logic: Proprietary multi-frame optical flow filters used to prevent bounding box flicker.
The Scientific Consequence

Regulatory clearances (FDA De Novo or 510(k)) evaluate device safety and clinical effectiveness on clinical outcomes—they do not mandate public release of model weights or code. Consequently, academic researchers cannot replicate commercial algorithms, and commercial vendors rarely adopt published academic models due to unverified training data and high false-alarm rates.
