Flaw 1: Video Frame Temporal Leakage in CVC-ClinicDB

30 Verified Links

In 2020, the PraNet benchmark protocol partitioned CVC-ClinicDB into 550 training frames and 62 test frames. However, CVC-ClinicDB consists of 612 consecutive frames extracted from approximately 31 endoscopic video sequences. Every single one of the 62 test frames originates from the identical patient video sequences used in training.

TRAINING SET (550 Frames) TEST SET (62 Frames) [Video Seq 04, Frame 180] ────── RANSAC Inliers ≥ 20 ─────> [Video Seq 04, Frame 181] [Video Seq 12, Frame 368] ──── Time Delta Δt ≈ 40ms ────> [Video Seq 12, Frame 369] Status: SEEN-DOMAIN INTERPOLATION (+12% to +15% Artificial Dice Inflation)
Impact on Medical AI: A model evaluated on ClinicDB test does not demonstrate generalized polyp detection; it interpolates memorized sequential transitions of polyps seen during backpropagation.
Flaw Deep Dive 01

Video Frame Temporal Leakage in CVC-ClinicDB

+12% to +15% Artificial Dice Inflation

The standard evaluation convention established by Fan et al. (MICCAI 2020) and inherited by more than 40 subsequent papers combines 900 images from Kvasir-SEG and 550 frames from CVC-ClinicDB into a 1,450-image training pool. Testing is conducted on 100 Kvasir-SEG test images, 62 CVC-ClinicDB test frames, and three unseen datasets (CVC-ColonDB, ETIS-Larib, and CVC-300).

Forensic Investigation: Near-Duplicate Link Analysis

Our forensic audit subjected all 612 ClinicDB frames to an automated dual-verification pipeline combining perceptual center-crop color correlation ($\ge 0.95$) and geometric feature matching using ORB descriptors with RANSAC affine consensus ($\ge 20$ geometric inliers).

The 30 Verified Near-Duplicate Links

The audit identified exactly 30 verified near-duplicate links directly connecting the 550 training frames to the 62 test frames. For instance:

  • train_180.png ↔ clinicdb_181.png: Consecutive video frames recorded ~40 milliseconds apart with 38 RANSAC inliers.
  • train_302.png ↔ clinicdb_304.png: 2 video frames apart, identical colon lumen lighting, 42 RANSAC inliers.
  • train_589.png ↔ clinicdb_590.png: Consecutive frames showing identical polyp stalk and margin.

Not a single frame in the 62-image test set originates from an unseen patient or novel endoscopy procedure.

Random Split Flaw (v1 Split Drift)

In standard machine learning implementations, researchers routinely take the 1,450-image pool and apply a uniform random split (e.g., 85% train, 15% val) without grouping by video sequence. In our initial v1 baseline:

  • Frame train_368.png assigned to Train; frame train_369.png assigned to Val.
  • Frame train_62.png assigned to Train; frame train_63.png assigned to Val.
  • 27 duplicate/sequence groups spanned both training and validation sets simultaneously.

This invalidates early-stopping and model checkpoint selection, as models are rewarded for memorizing sequence frames.

Why Metric Pooling is Scientific Malpractice

Because 58 out of 60 frames in CVC-300 are pixel-for-pixel copies of frames already present in CVC-ColonDB (specifically matching colondb_149.png through colondb_208.png to cvc300_149.png through cvc300_208.png), calculating an unweighted composite score such as "Mean Out-of-Distribution Dice = (ColonDB + ETIS + CVC-300) / 3" counts those 58 lesions twice. This inflates reported performance and artificially rewards models that happen to fit the color distribution of Hospital Clínic Barcelona.

Flaw Deep Dive 03

HyperKvasir: The 1,000/1,000 Duplicate Mask Finding

0 Extra Segmented Pairs

In 2020, Borgli et al. published the landmark HyperKvasir dataset in Scientific Data (Nature), an enormous archive containing 110,079 images and video frames from Bærum Hospital in Norway. Included in the repository is a dedicated folder containing 1,000 segmented polyp images and expert ground-truth masks.

Cryptographic Verification Proof

During the ChakraModel audit, an automated SHA-256 checksum scan was executed across all 1,000 segmented files in HyperKvasir against the 1,000 files in Kvasir-SEG (Jha et al., 2020):

For all i in {1, 2, ..., 1000}: SHA-256(HyperKvasir/segmented-images/polyp_[i].jpg) == SHA-256(Kvasir-SEG/images/cju[...].jpg) SHA-256(HyperKvasir/segmented-masks/polyp_[i].png) == SHA-256(Kvasir-SEG/masks/cju[...].png) RESULT: 1,000 / 1,000 Byte-for-Byte Identical Files (100.0% Match)

Correction to Published Literature: Numerous published machine learning papers have claimed to train on "an augmented dataset of 2,000 polyp images combining Kvasir-SEG and HyperKvasir." Our audit conclusively proves that these studies were unwittingly training on 1,000 duplicate pairs, effectively double-sampling the identical patients and masks. HyperKvasir provides zero additional segmented data beyond Kvasir-SEG.

Flaw Deep Dive 04

The False Alarm Blind Spot on Healthy Mucosa

Clinical Safety & Alarm Fatigue

The most dangerous blind spot in academic colonoscopy AI is the assumption of 100% polyp prevalence. Across all five canonical benchmark test sets (802 frames total), every single frame contains a polyp. Not a single image of normal, healthy colonic mucosa is evaluated.

The Academic Benchmark Mirage

  • Polyp Prevalence: 100.0% (802/802 frames).
  • Healthy Mucosa Frames: 0 (0.0%).
  • False Positive Measurement: Impossible.
  • Tested Capability: Delineating lesions known to exist.
  • Clinical Validity: Completely unverified for screening.

Real-World Clinical Reality

  • Polyp Prevalence: < 5.0% (< 500 frames per procedure).
  • Healthy Mucosa Frames: > 95.0% (> 10,000 frames per procedure).
  • Primary Clinical Hazard: Alarm fatigue from false alerts.
  • Distractors: Specular glints, bile pools, haustral folds.
  • Physician Reaction: Clinicians switch off annoying AI units.
