Colorectal Polyp Classification
Morphology, Histology & Optical Diagnosis

Not all polyps carry equal malignant risk. Master the macroscopic Paris classification, size stratification tiers, the conventional vs serrated neoplastic pathways, and high-magnification optical standards (Kudo, NICE, and JNET).

The Diminutive Paradox in Computer Vision

While diminutive polyps (≤5 mm) account for over 70% to 80% of all screening findings, their individual risk of invasive adenocarcinoma is fewer than 1 in 1,000 (<0.1%). Over 85% of incremental detections produced by commercial CADe systems are diminutive lesions, while detection of advanced adenomas (≥10 mm) remains clinically unchanged (RR 1.01).

Macroscopic Morphology

The Paris Classification of Superficial Lesions

Established in 2002, the Paris classification categorizes superficial gastrointestinal lesions (Type 0, confined to the mucosa and submucosa) based on height relative to endoscopic forceps jaws (2.5 mm).

Interactive Paris Morphological Selector

Click on any Paris lesion type below to inspect its macroscopic profile, physical height, submucosal invasion risk, and resection method:

Type 0-Ip: Pedunculated (Stalked)

Low Stalk Base Invasion (<2–3%)

Attached to the colonic wall by a distinct, slender fibromuscular stalk. The polypoid head is elevated far above the surrounding mucosal plane (>2.5 mm).

Endoscopic Resection Strategy:

Standard electrosurgical snare polypectomy across the mid-stalk. The stalk provides a natural surgical safety margin protecting the muscular wall.

Submucosal Invasion & AI Detection Reality:

Easiest lesion for deep learning detectors. Large protruding profile casts clear edge shadows that produce high IoU scores on academic benchmarks.

Invasion Risk: Low unless large head (>20 mm) exhibits invasive adenocarcinoma descending into stalk core (Haggitt Level 4).

Size Stratification

Polyp Size & Invasive Malignancy Risk

Physical diameter measured against open biopsy forceps (6–8 mm) or snare loops is the primary determinant of histology, cancer risk, and resection strategy.

Diminutive (≤5 mm) 70–80% of Polyps

Low Risk, High Volume

Overwhelmingly tubular adenomas or benign hyperplastic tags. Invasive cancer occurs in fewer than 1 in 1,000 (<0.1%).

CADe Impact: Represents >85% of all incremental AI detections. High cost for low cancer reduction.
Small (6 to 9 mm) 15–20% of Polyps

Intermediate Transition Zone

Tubular or tubulovillous adenomas, or right-sided SSLs. Invasive cancer risk is approximately 0.5%; high-grade dysplasia in 3% to 5%.

Management: Complete cold snare polypectomy with verified mucosal margin clearance.
Large / Advanced (≥10 mm) 5–10% of Polyps

High Malignant Potential

Invasive adenocarcinoma in 3.0% to 10.0%+ (surpassing 20–30% if >20 mm). High-grade dysplasia present in 10% to 25%.

CADe Trial Finding: CADe trials show zero statistically significant benefit for advanced adenomas (RR 1.01).
Molecular Pathogenesis

Conventional Adenoma vs Serrated Neoplastic Pathways

Colorectal carcinomas do not originate from a single molecular route. Understanding the distinct genetic drivers and endoscopic phenotypes explains why some polyps evade detection and progress rapidly to cancer.

1. Conventional Adenoma-Carcinoma Sequence

~70–80% of CRC

First described by Fearon and Vogelstein (1990). Driven by sequential chromosomal mutations over a 10 to 15-year latency period:

Normal Colonic Mucosa
↓ Bi-allelic loss of APC (Wnt pathway activation)
Early Tubular Adenoma (low-grade dysplasia)
↓ Activating mutation in KRAS (MAPK signaling) + 18q loss (SMAD4)
Tubulovillous / Villous Adenoma (high-grade dysplasia)
↓ TP53 loss + Chromosomal Instability (CIN)
Invasive Colorectal Adenocarcinoma
  • Anatomical Predilection: Distal colon (descending, sigmoid, and rectum).
  • Histology: Tubular (<25% villous), Tubulovillous (25–75% villous), and Villous (>75% villous).
  • Endoscopic Appearance: Distinct reddish polypoid lesions (0-Ip, 0-Is) with organized dark microvessels.

2. The Serrated Neoplastic Pathway

~15–30% of CRC

The alternative epigenetic cascade driven by oncogenic BRAF mutations and microsatellite instability (MSI). The primary driver of interval cancers:

Normal Colonic Crypts
↓ Activating BRAF V600E mutation
Hyperplastic Polyp (HP) / Microvesicular
↓ CpG Island Methylator Phenotype (CIMP-High)
Sessile Serrated Lesion (SSL/SSA)
↓ MLH1 promoter hypermethylation → Microsatellite Instability (MSI-H)
Serrated Adenocarcinoma (Rapid Progression)
  • Anatomical Predilection: Right / proximal colon (cecum, ascending colon).
  • Histology: Distorted basal crypts with lateral branching ("boot-shaped" or "inverted T-shaped").
  • Endoscopic Appearance: Flat (0-IIa/0-IIb), pale, indistinct borders, and concealed under a mucus cap.
Why Sessile Serrated Lesions (SSLs) Are the #1 AI Target

SSLs grow laterally with indistinct boundaries, match surrounding mucosa in color, and secrete mucus that traps bile and debris. While conventional adenomas take 10–15 years to become malignant, once an SSL acquires MLH1 hypermethylation, it can transform into an aggressive cancer in fewer than 2 to 3 years. Human endoscopists miss SSLs at rates up to 31% to 35%. AI systems specialized in serrated texture detection deliver the highest clinical value per unit of accuracy.

Real-Time Characterization

Optical Classification Systems: Kudo, NICE & JNET

Optical diagnosis allows endoscopists (and AI characterization engines, CADx) to predict histology in real time without waiting for laboratory biopsy results.

NICE Classification

NBI International Colorectal Endoscopic • Designed for non-magnifying Narrow Band Imaging

NICE Type 1 (Hyperplastic / Non-neoplastic) Leave
Color same as background; isolated faint thin vessels; uniform dark/white spots.
NICE Type 2 (Adenoma / Neoplastic) Snare Resection
Browner than background; thick microvessels surrounding white tubular structures.
NICE Type 3 (Deep Submucosal Cancer) Surgery
Dark brown/patchy white; disrupted, tortuous or avascular areas; amorphous pattern.

JNET Classification

Japan NBI Expert Team • Worldwide gold standard for magnifying NBI

JNET Type 1: Observe or Cold Snare
Invisible/faint vessels; regular dark spots. Histology: HP / SSL.
JNET Type 2A: Polypectomy / EMR
Regular caliber/distribution vessels; regular tubular pattern. Histology: Low-grade adenoma.
JNET Type 2B: En Bloc ESD
Variable caliber, irregular vessels; obscured pit pattern. High-grade dysplasia / shallow cancer (<1000 μm).
JNET Type 3: Surgical Resection
Interrupted thick vessels; avascular areas. Deep submucosal invasion (≥1000 μm).
